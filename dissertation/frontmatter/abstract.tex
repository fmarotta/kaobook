\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Sustainability transitions are multi-actor processes, yet transition research lacks adequate vocabulary and methods for systematically mapping how actors position themselves and relate to one another. Existing approaches either offer rich qualitative accounts of individual cases or apply predefined typologies that flatten the dynamism of role-taking in practice. Neither captures what happens at scale: how hundreds of organizations across an innovation ecosystem claim, contest, and perform intermediary roles through their self-presentations, media coverage, investment patterns, and visual identities.

This dissertation develops \textbf{multimodal cartography}---a methodology that bridges computational text analysis, network science, and visual analytics to systematically map role constellations across six data modalities: Wikipedia entries, organizational websites, news media coverage, investment records, social media presence, and visual materials. The approach embodies the bridging logic it studies: just as innovation intermediaries connect otherwise disconnected actors and knowledge domains, multimodal cartography connects otherwise siloed analytical traditions and data sources. Crucially, the methodology treats divergence between modalities as analytical signal rather than measurement noise---revealing how organizations navigate between claimed, attributed, and enacted identities.

\textbf{Theory.} We reconceptualize role constellations as complex adaptive systems, drawing on mechanisms of emergence, self-organization, and co-evolution to explain how micro-level role performances aggregate into meso-level constellation structures. The concept of \textit{interstitial pluralism} captures how actors strategically occupy bridging positions between established categories, turning structural ambiguity into adaptive advantage.

\textbf{Infrastructure.} Following Design Science Research principles, we develop \RoleBox---an open-source computational infrastructure implementing multimodal cartography. The toolkit includes modality-specific extraction pipelines, cross-modal triangulation algorithms, and supporting tools for agent-based simulation (RoleSim), discourse network analysis (RoleDNA), and computational literature review (RoleXray). All components are publicly available for replication and adaptation.

\textbf{Empirics.} Application to the European Institute of Innovation and Technology ecosystem---eight Knowledge and Innovation Communities spanning climate, health, digital, food, and other sectors---reveals systematic patterns. The \textit{connector gap} shows that bridging roles are far more often claimed than cross-modally performed. Business vocabularies dominate despite EIT's mission of balanced knowledge triangle integration. Recurring \textit{sociotypes} suggest field-level conventions in how organizations configure their role portfolios. Most strikingly, the \textit{breakdown gap} reveals complete absence of breakdown and exnovation roles across all five modalities---no organization claims, performs, or is attributed phase-out functions, suggesting innovation intermediaries are structurally oriented exclusively toward build-up dynamics despite the X-curve framework's demonstration that transitions require both acceleration \emph{and} managed decline.

These findings demonstrate that understanding how intermediaries position themselves in transition ecosystems requires computational infrastructure capable of systematic multimodal comparison---moving transition governance research from theory-driven typologies toward empirically-grounded cartography of role constellations, while revealing structural absences that current frameworks cannot even conceptualize.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} role constellations, interstitial actors, multimodal cartography, complex adaptive systems, sustainability transitions, EIT, computational social science, open science
